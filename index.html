Mental Health Self-Check

This brief survey helps you understand your current mental health and connects you with the right resources. There are no right or wrong answers — answer as honestly as you can.

Question 1 of 8 13%
Question 1 of 8

Over the past two weeks, how often have you felt down, depressed, or hopeless?

Think about how often this has been the case, not just today.

Question 2 of 8

Over the past two weeks, how often have you had little interest or pleasure in doing things you used to enjoy?

This might include hobbies, spending time with friends, or activities you once looked forward to.

Question 3 of 8

Over the past two weeks, how often have you felt nervous, anxious, or on edge?

This includes general worry, unease, or a sense that something bad might happen.

Question 4 of 8

Over the past two weeks, how often have you been unable to stop or control worrying?

For example, worrying about school, the future, relationships, or things that might go wrong.

Question 5 of 8

How has your sleep been over the past two weeks?

Changes in sleep are often an early sign of mental health challenges.

Question 6 of 8

How would you describe your ability to concentrate on schoolwork or daily tasks recently?

Consider whether you have found it harder than usual to focus or follow through on things.

Question 7 of 8

How often have you withdrawn from social situations or avoided people you care about?

This includes canceling plans, not responding to messages, or isolating yourself.

Question 8 of 8

Have you had any thoughts of harming yourself or that you would be better off not being here?

This is a safe space. Your answer helps us point you to the most appropriate support. There is no wrong answer.

Is This an Emergency?

Answer a few quick questions to understand how serious your situation might be and what kind of help is right for you.

?

Are you safe right now?

Are you currently in a physically safe place, with no immediate risk of harm to yourself or others?

?

Are you having thoughts of hurting yourself or suicide?

Even passive thoughts (like "I wish I wasn't here") count. There is no wrong answer.

?

Is this significantly disrupting your daily life?

Think about school, sleep, eating, friendships, or your ability to function day-to-day.

?

Has this been going on for more than two weeks?

Occasional hard days are normal. Consistent struggles lasting weeks are worth addressing with professional support.

!

Please reach out for help right now

You deserve immediate support. Please contact a crisis service — free, confidential, and available 24/7.

Call 988 Now Text 741741
!

You are not alone — and help is here

Having thoughts of self-harm is a signal to get support now. Please reach out to a crisis line — they will not judge you, and they are there to listen.

Call or Text 988 Text HOME to 741741

Also consider talking to a school counselor or trusted adult today.

↑

You would benefit from professional support

What you are going through is real and deserves real help. A therapist or counselor can make a significant difference. You do not need to be in crisis to reach out.

+

Self-care and monitoring is a great start

It sounds like things are manageable right now. Self-help tools, staying connected, and checking in with yourself regularly can really help. If things change, support is always here.
